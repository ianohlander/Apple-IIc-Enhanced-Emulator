The 3 Legal Pillars of Retro Emulation

💾 1. Firmware ROM Copyright

The Law: Original Apple ROM binary dumps (Applesoft BASIC, 1984 //c System ROM) remain protected by Apple Inc. copyright. Distributing or selling original Apple binary dumps is illegal.

Our Safe Harbor: Apple //c Ultra uses a 100% Clean-Room Synthesized BIOS (generateDefaultApple2cRom()) written from scratch. Zero proprietary Apple bytes are bundled!
⚙️ 2. Hardware Emulation Legality

The Law: Emulating CPU instruction sets (65C02), ALU logic, memory-mapped I/O, sound synthesis chips (AY-3-8910), and video timings is 100% legal under established US court precedent (Sony v. Connectix, Sony v. Bleem!).

Our Safe Harbor: The entire TypeScript/JS emulation engine is original software authored by Ian Ohlander.
🏷️ 3. Trademark & Nominative Fair Use

The Law: "Apple", "Apple II", "Apple //c", and "ProDOS" are registered trademarks of Apple Inc. You cannot use them as the primary commercial product name for commercial sales.

Our Safe Harbor: Use descriptive Nominative Fair Use (e.g. "Ultra 6502 Lab (Apple II Compatible)") paired with formal disclaimer notices.

Commercial Monetization Boundaries

✅ What You CAN Legally Monetize Freely
  • Your Original Software & Compiler: The C# and Java AOT compiler engine, 65C02 emulator, and debugger toolchain.
  • Textbooks & Courseware: The 9-chapter Systems Engineering textbook, university lab manuals, lecture slides, and solution keys.
  • Original Video Games: Retro Breakout, Dungeon Crawler, Raycaster, and new games authored using your SDK.
  • Developer Extensions: VS Code marketplace extensions, memory profilers, and disk utilities.
  • Institutional Licensing: University department lab support agreements and courseware packages ($499/dept).
❌ What You Must NEVER Bundle or Sell
  • Proprietary Apple ROM Dumps: Do NOT sell or bundle original 1984 Apple //c ROM binaries (342-0272, 342-0033).
  • Commercial Games Owned by 3rd Parties: Do not bundle commercial disk images (e.g. Broderbund's Lode Runner or Choplifter) in paid products.
  • Branding as Official Apple Hardware: Do not brand commercial products as an official Apple product.
