The 3 Legal Pillars of Retro Emulation

💾 1. Firmware ROM Copyright

The Law: Original Apple ROM binary dumps (Applesoft BASIC, 1984 //c System ROM) remain protected by Apple Inc. copyright. Distributing or selling original Apple binary dumps is illegal.

Our Safe Harbor: Apple //c Ultra uses a 100% Clean-Room Synthesized BIOS (generateDefaultApple2cRom()) written from scratch. Zero proprietary Apple bytes are bundled!
⚙️ 2. Hardware Emulation Legality

The Law: Emulating CPU instruction sets (65C02), ALU logic, memory-mapped I/O, sound synthesis chips (AY-3-8910), and video timings is 100% legal under established US court precedent (Sony v. Connectix, Sony v. Bleem!).

The Law: "Apple", "Apple II", "Apple //c", and "ProDOS" are registered trademarks of Apple Inc. You cannot use them as the primary commercial product name for commercial sales.

Deep-Dive: How Clean-Room ROMs Execute DSK Images, Type-Ins & Games

The 4 Architectural Layers Enabling 100% Binary Compatibility Without Proprietary Apple Code

A common question in retro systems engineering is: If the emulator uses a clean-room synthesized BIOS instead of Apple's proprietary ROM dumps, how can vintage disk images (.DSK), magazine type-in programs, and complex arcade games run seamlessly?

📐 Figure L.1: Clean-Room Compatibility & Execution Topology ABI & Hardware Layering
1. Unalterable ABI & Vector Jump Tables ($F800–$FFFF)

In 1977, Steve Wozniak established fixed vector entry points for monitor I/O. Any software calling JSR $FDED (Character Out) or JSR $FD1B (Key In) relies on the functional contract (Accumulator contains character, screen updates, registers preserved).

Our clean-room ROM implements this exact ABI contract. The caller doesn't care what byte sequence is inside ROM, only that $FDED prints the character and returns!

2. Disk Images Carry Their Own Operating Systems in RAM

When you boot a floppy (.DSK) or 32MB hard disk, the ROM's only job is executing the 256-byte bootstrap jump at $C600 or $C700 to load Track 0 Sector 0.

The diskette immediately loads DOS 3.3 (RWTS @ $B7E8) or ProDOS 2.4.2 (MLI @ $BF00) directly into RAM ($9D00–$BFFF). From that point onward, the software executes out of RAM, completely independent of the motherboard ROM!

3. 95% of Games Bypass ROM and Write Directly to Silicon

Vintage commercial games (Choplifter, Lode Runner, Prince of Persia, Breakout) never called ROM routines during gameplay because ROM routines were too slow for 60 FPS animation.

Instead, games write directly to hardware softswitches ($C000 Keyboard, $C030 Speaker, $C050-$C057 Video switches, $C0EC-$C0EF Stepper). Because our hardware emulation is cycle-accurate, games run with 100% fidelity.

4. Applesoft BASIC Tokenizer & Zero Page Pointer Mapping

For typed-in or magazine BASIC programs, our engine automatically parses ASCII text into standard Applesoft bytecode tokens ($80–$EA) and initializes standard Zero Page pointers ($67–$68 Program Start, $AF–$B0 Program End).

This allows typed-in programs from Compute!, inCider, and Nibble to run natively without requiring Microsoft/Apple's copyrighted BASIC interpreter ROM binary.
